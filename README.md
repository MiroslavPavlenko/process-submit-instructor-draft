## Purpose

Graduating with a computer science or software engineering degree is an important starting point for studios like Scopely, who hire applicants who can demonstrate real-world shipping work before graduation. This guide outlines the best practices to follow between now and graduation to make sure that your portfolio, abilities, and professional presence reflect your development as a full-stack game engineer. Scopely's full-stack engineer position lists Unity and C# skills, backend API knowledge, and familiarity with cloud platforms, which are skills students may need to build outside of regular coursework.[^1] Following these practices helps you show recruiters clear evidence of what you can build, not only what classes you have completed.

---

## Build Real Projects Outside of Class

**Why it matters:** Course projects tell a recruiter you can follow instructions; personal projects tell them you can think independently and ship something real.

**What to do:** Participate in at least two game jams per year on itch.io, publish every project to a public GitHub repository with a descriptive README, and treat each one as a portfolio piece, not a throwaway weekend exercise.[^2][^3]

## Sharpen Your Technical Interview Skills Early

**Why it matters:** Game studio technical rounds test data structures, algorithms, and problem-solving under pressure. Waiting until senior year to start practicing can make the process more stressful.

**What to do:** Solve at least three LeetCode problems per week starting in your sophomore year, prioritize medium-difficulty problems in arrays, graphs, and dynamic programming, and time yourself to simulate real interview conditions.[^4]

## Make Your Online Presence Work for You

**Why it matters:** A LinkedIn profile and GitHub profile help a recruiter quickly understand what you are building and what skills you want to use.

**What to do:** Pin your two strongest Unity or backend projects to your GitHub profile, keep your commit history active with consistent weekly pushes, and write a LinkedIn headline that names your target role and core skills rather than just listing your degree.[^3][^5]

## Get Hands-On With Cloud Platforms

**Why it matters:** Live-service games run on cloud infrastructure, and a fullstack role at Scopely expects exposure to GCP, AWS, or Azure before your first day.[^1]

**What to do:** Complete one personal cloud project each semester on a free tier account. Deploy a REST API, connect it to a database, document the architecture, and add it to your portfolio.[^6][^7][^8] This gives you a practical example to discuss when a recruiter or interviewer asks about backend experience.

## Target a Game Studio Internship Before Senior Year

**Why it matters:** An internship at a game studio, even in a role adjacent to your target, signals that you understand the production pace, cross-functional collaboration, and ship-it culture that separates game development from standard enterprise software work.

**What to do:** Start applying to summer internships in October of your sophomore year because many major studios open applications months before the start date.[^9] Track each application, update your resume after every new project, and ask for referrals or informational interviews when possible.

### Staying on Track

Set a calendar reminder each November and April to review your GitHub activity, count your shipped projects, and update your LinkedIn with anything new. Starting early makes the process easier because each project, commit, and interview practice session builds on the last one.
